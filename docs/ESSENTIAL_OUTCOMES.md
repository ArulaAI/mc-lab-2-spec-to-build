# Essential Outcomes Card

**Write-protected.** Keep this open during the session.

## What is graded

Engineering outcomes and evidence. Not whether your screen matches the facilitator's.

> Model wording, ordering and formatting will differ between people, and between two runs by the
> same person. That is normal and expected. We grade what you concluded, what you can show for it,
> and what you refused to do — never whether your response looks like anyone else's.

If your agent phrases something differently, finds the same problem by another route, or returns
its findings in a different order, you are not behind.

## What this card does and does not tell you

It tells you **which artifacts must exist and what good evidence looks like in them**. It does not
tell you what you will find. Naming the findings would hand you the conclusions the session exists
to have you reach, and would make the ledger a transcription exercise.

So: if you are looking here for what is wrong with the two services, it is not here on purpose.

## The eight outcomes

By the end of the session your workspace should show:

1. **A context ledger with reconciled rows, not a list of observations.** Each row carries a claim,
   a citation you can click, and a ruling. A row without a ruling is a note; a ruling without a
   citation is an opinion.
2. **At least one row where the two audits genuinely disagreed**, resolved by your ruling with the
   evidence you based it on — not by which agent sounded more certain.
3. **At least one unknown still unresolved** rather than filled in with a plausible default, and
   recorded well enough that the next person can see what would settle it.
4. **A specification that passes its readiness gate with its out-of-scope list intact** — and
   nothing excluded got built.
5. **A plan that defines repository boundaries and a compatibility rationale**, not just a task
   list, plus one agent contract per repository stating the acceptance criteria that repository owns.
6. **Every acceptance criterion owned by exactly one repository**, and each contract declaring
   whether it expects a diff at all.
7. **Each repository verified on its own terms** — its own build and tests run, recorded, and
   attributable to that repository rather than to the pair.
8. **Pair verification green**, and you can say what that does and does not prove.

## Two things that are outcomes, not failures

**A "no diff" result, with evidence, is a real outcome.** Concluding that a repository is already
correct and proving it is engineering work. So is declining to change something because it is out
of scope, and recording why you declined.

Which repositories that applies to, if any, is yours to determine. This is a statement about how
outcomes are scored, not a hint about where to apply it.

**An unresolved question left visible beats a plausible answer invented to close it.** In payments,
guessing a threshold or a default is a business decision you were not authorised to make. Surfacing
it is the correct move, and it is scored as such.

## What this lab does not claim

The lab proves the represented seam locally. It does not claim the wider PGS refund capability is
production-ready, and pair verification does not replace integration testing or release governance.
